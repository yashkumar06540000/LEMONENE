THE CONCEPT · 01

Turning a peel into a starting point.

Citrus peels are often left over after fruit is processed. Lemonene asks whether some of that overlooked material could become a useful feedstock for future fuel research.

What is limonene?

A familiar scent with an interesting chemistry.

Limonene is a naturally occurring compound found in the peels of citrus fruits such as oranges and lemons. It is part of what gives their oils a bright, recognizable aroma.

The Lemonene project is exploring an early-stage idea: recover limonene from citrus peel waste and investigate whether it could be converted or used in a fuel pathway.

The goal is to learn what is technically, environmentally, and economically viable—not to claim a finished fuel exists today.

From waste to research

A possible path, one question at a time.

Each step would need careful testing. Lemonene is a concept, and turning it into a practical fuel would require research and validation.

01

Collect

Explore sources of citrus peel by-products from food processing and other local supply chains.

02

Recover

Investigate safe, efficient ways to separate useful compounds from the peel material.

03

Evaluate

Study conversion options, fuel performance, life-cycle impacts, and whether the process makes sense at scale.

The bigger picture

Make better use of what we already have.

Lemonene starts with a simple question: could a citrus by-product become part of a smarter energy story?

Curiosity comes first. Sustainable fuel depends on more than its source. A credible solution must also consider how the feedstock is collected, how much energy processing uses, what emissions result, and how it compares with alternatives.
